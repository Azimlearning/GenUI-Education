# Codex Community Hackathon KL 2026 — Team Experience Notes

Raw account of the team's (DANG WANGI, project "Synapse") experience during the event, used as source material for the participant feedback form.

## Arrival and setup
- Directions to the venue were a bit confusing, but the team made it in time.
- Registration was easy.
- The team was given a QR code for project deliverable submission, but it wasn't explained until this point how to actually submit. Some required deliverables were also never mentioned in advance, so the team wasn't prepared for them.

## Venue conditions
- No Wi-Fi at the venue — the team had to use their own mobile hotspot for the entire event.
- Not enough plugs/charging ports/extensions. Had to charge a laptop at another team's station and leave it there, only returning once it was full to continue work at their own station.

## Event schedule and format
- The event itself started late; the opening/VIP segment was not well managed.
- The event was delayed heavily throughout the day.
- Original stated format: 2-phase judging — all groups present, then top 10 chosen to present in front, then top 5 winners chosen.
- The calling order was supposed to follow form-submission time, which was fine as just a queue mechanism, but in practice the actual order kept changing randomly throughout the day (e.g. told they were group 22, then later 40, then suddenly other groups were called ahead of them out of nowhere). This made it very hard to plan bathroom breaks, prayer breaks, or any short break at all, since the team always had to stay alert in case their name was called.
- The presentation format changed multiple times during the day:
  1. First few groups: 10-minute presentation not including Q&A, presented to two separate groups of judges (2 total presentations).
  2. Changed to presenting to all judges at once (in front).
  3. Changed so the time limit included the Q&A session.
  4. Total time kept reducing.
  5. Q&A was removed entirely.
  6. Time kept reducing further until this team's slot was only 3 minutes total — no Q&A, compared to the first groups' 10 minutes + separate Q&A. Key pitch details had to be skipped.
- Mid-event, some groups had to relocate to another venue; that venue turned out to be too small, so everyone had to move back to the main hall — this happened while some groups had already started presenting.
- After all groups presented, the organizers did not select a top 10 as originally planned — they went straight to announcing the top 5.
- The event was scheduled to end at 5pm but actually ended at 6:30pm, without even completing the originally planned final phase.

## Closing ceremony
- Poorly managed: no proper emcee or floor manager.
- Slides were very basic and didn't even include the winning team's name, causing confusion (including how to pronounce it).
- A sponsorship segment was scheduled after closing, by which point most attendees had already left.

## Fairness concerns
- Judging/evaluation did not appear to follow the stated evaluation criteria consistently.
- Every group effectively presented under different conditions (different time limits, different Q&A availability, different judge setups), making comparison unfair.
- The eventual top 5 winning teams were the teams that presented earliest, who clearly had the advantage of more time, a Q&A session, full demo, and undivided judge attention/engagement compared to later teams.

## What was good
- The judges were professional — they even approached the team after the event to ask questions.
- The organizing committee tried to help find a charging port for the team (although ultimately unsuccessful).
- The food was good, as was the pre-event experience.
