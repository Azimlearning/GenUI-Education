# Grand Final Deliverables and Prep Plan

Three deliverables on 29 October 2026, confirmed by Dr. Cai on 20 Aug:

1. A **10 minute project presentation**
2. A **5 minute Q&A**
3. A **booth demonstration** at the conference venue, staffed through the day

Plus the award ceremony that afternoon. The organiser also asks teams to wear university uniforms or
team shirts and to bring a banner or flag carrying UTP's identity.

This is a different format from the preliminary round, which was an 8 minute video and a written
description. Nothing from the prelim transfers unedited.

---

## What the judges score

The four criteria from the competition are equally weighted, so the presentation should visibly hit
all four rather than spending nine minutes on the architecture.

| Criterion | Focus | What to show in 10 minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Innovation and Novelty | Originality of concept | Generative UI: the interface is built on the spot for the student's specific misconception, not selected from prebuilt screens |
| Technical Readiness Level | Build quality and execution | A live system, running, on real Form 4 and Form 5 content. Not slides of a system |
| Commercial Viability | Market potential | Who pays, what it costs to serve a student, why a school or ministry adopts it |
| Educational Significance | Pedagogical impact | The rural and lower income lab access gap, and the misconception a quiz never catches |

## The 10 minute shape

A workable split, adjust after the first rehearsal against a clock:

| Minutes | Segment | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 1:30 | The problem, concretely | A named school without lab equipment, a student who scores well on osmosis while believing water moves to wherever there is more water |
| 1:30 to 3:00 | Why a fixed interface cannot solve it | Hundreds of experiments, every student confused differently. This is the setup for the novelty claim |
| 3:00 to 7:00 | **Live demo** | Type a real student question, watch the agents diagnose and build the simulation. This is the segment that wins or loses the round |
| 7:00 to 8:30 | How it works | Agent flow and generative UI, one diagram, no code |
| 8:30 to 10:00 | Impact and path forward | Reach, cost per student, adoption route, what is next |

Rules of thumb:
- Rehearse to 9:30, not 10:00. Overrunning in front of a chair with a timer is avoidable.
- One presenter for continuity plus one driving the demo is usually stronger than four handoffs.
- Assume the venue wifi fails. Have an offline build and a recorded demo video as fallback, in that
  order.

## Q&A, 5 minutes

Prepare short answers, 30 to 45 seconds each, for the questions that always come:

- How is this different from a chatbot with a plotting library attached?
- What stops the AI generating a physically wrong simulation? What is the validation path?
- Has any real student used it? What happened?
- What does one student cost you to serve per month?
- Why Malaysia and KSSM specifically, and does it generalise?
- Who owns the content, and how does it stay aligned to the syllabus when the syllabus changes?
- How do you measure that a misconception was actually corrected, not just covered?

Assign a primary answerer per topic before the day so there is no pause and no two people starting
at once.

## Booth, full day 29 Oct

The booth runs the entire conference day, which is a longer commitment than the presentation and
needs a roster.

- **Kit:** roll-up banner, UTP flag or banner, printed one-pager with a QR code to the demo, laptops
  plus chargers and a European plug adapter (type C or F), a mobile hotspot, and a power strip.
  Baggage allowance on the chosen fare is 25 kg checked, which the briefing notes was picked partly
  to carry the banner and roll-up.
- **Roster:** minimum two people on the booth at all times, rotating, so nobody misses the keynotes
  or the award ceremony. Build the roster before departure.
- **The 60 second pitch:** a passerby gives you one minute. Write and rehearse a spoken version that
  ends with an offer to try it themselves on the laptop.
- **Capture:** collect names and emails of anyone interested. Judges, other university advisors and
  conference attendees are the reason a booth is worth staffing.
